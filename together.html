Build RPMS in the open, with us

RPMS belongs to the programs and people it serves. OpenRPMS builds each IHS release in the open, so that sites, IHS, partners and developers can look at the same thing, check it, and improve it together. This page says how a release is made, what it records, and where you can take part.

How a release is built

  1. From IHS's public files. The build starts from the last complete RPMS database IHS released under FOIA and installs every KIDS patch IHS has released since, in order. Every input is checksummed before it is used.
  2. Stock first. Every way a release differs from stock RPMS is registered, with its reason, and whether stock was tried without it. Defects found in the public release are recorded with their evidence.
  3. Gated. A release publishes only when it reaches the IHS certified patch level, every certified package is installed and current, and real clients sign on and get the answers they expect.
  4. Published with its evidence. Each release carries what it contains, layer by layer, where every input came from, every RPC it registers, and the record of each check, as files anyone can read without running anything.

It is built for two engines: IRIS for Health, the engine IHS certifies RPMS on, and YottaDB, which is open source. The YottaDB line is not certified today, and we intend to pursue it.

See the releases and the build repository.

Where you can take part

  • Clinic and program staff. Walk your role's work on the demo and tell us where it differs from how your clinic works. The needs and journeys you check are on People first.
  • Site IT staff. Run a release and report what you find: a platform it has not met, a step that did not end the way the page says.
  • Developers. Build on the RPCs with rpms-rpc and lakeraven-ehr, and send fixes as pull requests.
  • IHS and partners. Review a release's evidence, and the defects it records in the public release. A fix that IHS carries in its own patches is the best outcome, because every site then takes it the usual way, by KIDS. We would welcome building releases together, in the open.

What we hold to

  • The work comes first: a change is judged by what it does for the person doing the work.
  • Stock RPMS first, and every difference written down.
  • Synthetic data only, everywhere in the open.
  • Every release reproducible from public sources, so no single host or sponsor is a single point of failure.
