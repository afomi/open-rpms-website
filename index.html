RPMS, built in the open, starting with the people who use it.

A health program runs on work people do every day: registering a patient, booking a visit, seeing the patient, billing for the care. The Resource and Patient Management System (RPMS) has supported that work for the Indian Health Service for more than 40 years. OpenRPMS starts with the people who do the work and what they need, and builds each IHS release of RPMS in the open, with the evidence that it works.

You can try it in a browser today, run it yourself, and build on it.

One release, two engines

Each release carries the same RPMS packages at the same IHS patch level, and the same demo clinic. You choose the engine that fits your site.

RPMS on YottaDB

  • Open source, free to run: one container image.
  • The chart's broker, iCare's broker and the terminal.
  • No BPRM web registration and scheduling.
  • Not certified today; we intend to pursue it.

RPMS on IRIS

  • Bring your own InterSystems IRIS for Health license.
  • Every broker, plus the BMW database BPRM reads.
  • IRIS is the engine IHS certifies RPMS on.
  • Community Edition runs it for evaluation.

Why this exists

RPMS serves one of the most underserved patient populations in the United States. It has done so reliably for decades, in low-bandwidth, resource-constrained environments, at facilities ranging from a two-room rural clinic to a 200-bed area hospital.

What makes it valuable is the operational knowledge in its design: how a clinic's work really flows, and the hard-won decisions about what health IT needs to do under pressure.

OpenRPMS carries that knowledge forward in three ways. It describes the work from the people who do it. It builds each IHS release of RPMS reproducibly, from public files, with the evidence that it works. And it gives clinics, IHS, partners and developers one place to look at the same thing and improve it together.
